01 — Headline work

Building a neutral-atom quantum computer, from scratch.

Most high schoolers don't spend their weekends aligning optics for atom traps. I do — and this fall I'm cleared to start building a quantum computer in the TJ labs.

Neutral-atom quantum computers hold individual atoms in tightly focused beams of laser light — optical tweezers — and use them as qubits. I've been building that stack myself: designing atomic traps for rubidium and cesium, and bringing functional optical tweezers into their first experimental testing.

My goal is a version of an optical quantum computer that's financially practical for high schools and small universities, yet large enough to run real code. It's the kind of tangible scaling problem I find impossible to put down.

05 — About

Who I am

I'm a high-school student at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology, where I study optical quantum computing — and spend my weekends aligning optics for atom traps I build myself.

Most of what I do starts the same way: a hard problem, a pile of parts, and the stubborn belief that a student can build the real thing. That's taken me from neutral-atom tweezers to a liquid-fuel rocket, an accessibility startup, two finance papers, and a provisional patent.

When I'm not in a lab or a CAD file, I'm playing viola, 3-D printing something, cooking, or arguing a point in Model UN.

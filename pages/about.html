Who I am

I'm a high-school student at Thomas Jefferson High School for Science and Technology, where I study optical quantum computing — and spend my weekends aligning optics for atom traps I build myself.

Most of what I do starts the same way: a hard problem, a pile of parts, and the stubborn belief that a student can build the real thing. That instinct has taken me from neutral-atom optical tweezers to a liquid-fueled rocket, an accessibility startup, two finance papers with a university professor, and a provisional patent for a device that repairs pipes from the inside.

I'm just as interested in the people side of building. I've captained a robotics team across all of its build, programming, and outreach divisions, served as a crisis director and webmaster in Model UN, and tutored higher-level math and English — and I've found that the projects that actually ship are the ones where the team communicates well.

When I'm not in a lab or a CAD file, I'm playing viola, 3-D printing something, cooking, public speaking, or reading far too much news.
